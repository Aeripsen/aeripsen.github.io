imammahdi.ca

A 13 page bilingual English and Farsi site for the Imam Mahdi Islamic Centre, a registered Canadian charity in Thornhill, Ontario. It has a live generative night sky that renders the real moon phase and the real position of the sun, prayer times computed to a stated astronomical method with a public page explaining it, a from scratch Persian and Gregorian calendar, an ambient soundscape synthesized in the browser, and a Python content system that turns the mosque's Telegram posts into the site's events.

My role, stated exactly

This is volunteer work. I rebuilt the current public site, I own the repository and the deployment, and I maintain it week to week. I did not build the organisation, and I did not write every page that has ever existed on that domain: one page is maintained separately by another volunteer, and there were contributors before me.

The sky

One Canvas 2D engine drives the hero of every page.

It composites a three layer parallax starfield of roughly 800 to 1400 seeded stars with a magnitude and colour distribution, a Milky Way band built from seeded luminance blobs, and a slowly rotating Islamic girih pattern traced as a constellation. Below that sits a mosque skyline silhouette with an onion dome, a hilal finial and window glow blooms, procedural cumulus and cirrus banks rendered in separate sunlit crown and shaded base passes, and rare shooting stars.

  • The moon is drawn at its true phase

    Synodic age is computed from a known new moon epoch, and the terminator is rendered by compositing an offscreen canvas, with maria, earthshine and craters on top. It is not a decorative crescent. If you look up, it matches.

  • The sky and the prayer times cannot disagree

    The sun is placed at its real position on a horizon arc, and the page's whole colour temperature, html[data-phase] as night, dawn, day or dusk, is derived from the same live prayer time data that renders the prayer cards. One source, so the picture and the numbers can never drift apart.

  • Performance, because the congregation is on low end Android

    devicePixelRatio capped at 2. Static layers pre rendered once per resize into offscreen canvases, so a frame is three drawImage calls. IntersectionObserver plus the Page Visibility API to stop work nobody can see. An FPS watchdog that sheds the Milky Way and freezes the constellation rotation when the frame rate drops. The reject knob on this site's front page runs on the same discipline: a pre rendered base, one drawImage per animated frame, and no loop at all while nothing moves.

  • Reduced motion is honoured properly

    Not "animations run slower". The canvas falls back to a single static painting.

Prayer times

The times, and a public page explaining why they are those times.

Times come from the Aladhan API using calculation method 7, the Institute of Geophysics at the University of Tehran, with Fajr at 17.7 degrees, Maghrib at 4.5 and Isha at 14, and midnightMode=1 for Jafari midnight, all behind a window.PRAYER_API abstraction so the source can be swapped.

  • A 24 hour SVG dial

    An elapsed day arc, the six daily times plotted as markers at their true angular positions, a next prayer glow, a live countdown in the ring centre, and a tinted disc that follows the real phase of day. Reference times, meaning sunrise, sunset and midnight, are visually distinguished from the five prayers so the dial does not imply six obligations.

  • A Qibla compass computed, not looked up

    Great circle initial bearing calculated in JavaScript from the mosque's coordinates, drawn as a compass rose with a gold needle.

  • A method explainer, with an instrument on it

    A public page for why this centre's times differ from two nearby centres while matching the mainstream Iranian tables. It carries a live four method comparison table, an interactive SVG sun depth control (a 0 to 20 degree slider showing horizon redness fading past the Maghrib threshold), and a numeric cross check: pointing the same engine at Tehran reproduced published Iranian timetables to the minute. The research behind it is a 27 source cited brief.

Scope

I implemented the calculation method the Centre follows and made it transparent. I am not a religious authority and the page does not rule on anything. It states which method is in use, shows the parameters, and lets a visitor compare.

Bilingual, properly

Full RTL using CSS logical properties, Vazirmatn scoped to Persian glyphs with unicode-range, the <html lang> attribute tracking the language toggle, and Persian-Indic digits rendered through Intl with the fa-IR locale.

The calendar is a from scratch month grid carrying both the Jalali and the Gregorian date in every cell, verified against the mosque's own printed posters. In Farsi the week starts on Saturday, so the grid re lays itself through a MutationObserver on the language toggle and puts Saturday rightmost in RTL.

Detail that cost me an afternoon and is worth knowing: logical insets flip in RTL, so any fixed corner UI has to be checked in both directions or it ends up under your thumb in one language and across the screen in the other.

Content is data driven from events.json, thisweek.json and guests.json: recurring programs, dated one off events that retire themselves after their date, a weekly overlay for the prayer leader and lecture topic, and a speakers archive. Every read goes through one accessor behind a DATA_MODE switch, so a real backend can be dropped in without a rewrite.

Accessibility throughout: skip links, :focus-visible rings, aria-current on navigation, aria-live readouts on the interactive instruments, and every animation gated on prefers-reduced-motion.

Audio

An ambient soundscape with no audio files, at first.

Opt in, built with the Web Audio API: brown noise shaped by a gusting lowpass with two slow LFOs for wind, bandpassed noise bands plus scheduled sine chirps for a stream, a triangle wave bird phrase scheduler, and gated pulse train oscillators for crickets, all through a high shelf filter and a DynamicsCompressor.

The mix follows the sky. Presets change with the solar phase until a visitor touches a slider, after which their own mix is remembered in localStorage. Later I replaced two synth voices with CC0 and public domain field recordings from Wikimedia Commons, trimmed and loudness normalized with ffmpeg to roughly 325 KB and 450 KB .m4a files, fetched lazily and crossfaded over the synth, which stays as the instant start and offline fallback. Everything ramps over seconds, and muting calls AudioContext.suspend() so an idle tab costs nothing.

Content system

Three Python tools I wrote, all doing unglamorous work that has to be right every week.

  • telegram_pull.py

    A standard library only scraper of the mosque's public Telegram channels that maintains a growing archive: a JSON index keyed by channel and post, images downloaded under stable filenames, and a browsable Markdown history, with backward pagination to seed the past. It exists because Telegram's CDN URLs expire, so posters have to be captured and self hosted or the site's history rots.

  • telegram_harvest.py

    Recovers real .mp4 URLs from Telegram's embed pages and downloads them with HTTP Range requests, de duplicating album posts.

  • draft_events.py

    Drafts bilingual event records through a router that tries free tier models in order, so the routine extraction costs nothing to run every week. Drafts only. Wording, especially religious wording, is mine.

Media handling with ffmpeg (H.264 re encode with -movflags +faststart, poster frame extraction) and Pillow for thumbnails. One event's registration link only existed inside a QR code on a low resolution poster, so I decoded it out with OpenCV.

Ship and harden

  • Continuous deployment

    A GitHub Actions workflow rsyncs over SSH to cPanel hosting using a dedicated deploy key held as a repository secret, and stamps a cache buster on the way out.

  • A hand written service worker

    Network first for navigations and for JSON data, so a visitor never gets a stale announcement, and old caches are purged on every build stamp. With manifest.json, the site is installable.

  • Server configuration

    Apache and LiteSpeed rules for long lived immutable caching on fingerprinted assets, short revalidated caching on HTML, gzip, the standard security response headers (Strict-Transport-Security, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy), and rules denying source and backup file types.

  • A security hardening pass on the live host

    Filesystem permission remediation across the document root, access control on non public directories, credential hygiene, and a backdoor and malware audit. Every change made reversibly and verified against the live site afterwards. Deliberately described at this level and no lower. This is a real charity's infrastructure, and the specifics are not portfolio material.

  • Findable and shareable

    Open Graph and Twitter card metadata per page, and JSON-LD Mosque structured data.

The standard

The page I had to rewrite.

I wrote a page explaining the symbolism of the site's geometric patterns. A cited research pass came back and showed my first draft attributed sectarian meanings to Islamic geometric patterns that historians do not support. The published version uses only cross sectarian, historically grounded language.

On a community site, being wrong in public is the actual failure mode. So changeable facts, and especially Islamic calendar dates, are cite or cut: verified against the organisation's own published sources, with the date recorded.

Planned, not built

Three things are on the roadmap and are not shipped: a donations backend (evaluating Zeffy for zero fee processing with automatic Canadian tax receipts, alongside PayPal Giving Fund), a Directus headless CMS behind the existing DATA_MODE switch so staff can edit content without touching code, and staying a PWA rather than building a native app.
