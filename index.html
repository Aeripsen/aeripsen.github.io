The idea

A classifier that answers every question is a classifier that lies on the hard ones. The knob above is the whole argument: let the model decline, and you can trade how much it covers against how often it is right, on a curve you measured rather than a number you hoped for.

That is the contribution of my SECRYPT paper, and it is the spine of everything on this site: a network intrusion detector, a card fraud detector, and the four protocol IoT system I am building now all expose the same control. The interesting engineering is not the model. It is knowing which flows you are allowed to be confident about, and proving it on a split that cannot leak.

If your team runs a fraud queue, a SOC, or a risk model that pages someone at 3am, this tradeoff is already your job: every alert spends analyst time, every miss costs more, and the worst failures are the confident wrong answers. I would rather hand you the whole measured curve than quote you its best point.

How I work

I run more parallel work than one person should be able to, because I built the scaffolding to keep it honest.

On the research and on the sites, I fan work out across a fleet of agents and hold the result to contracts I write: one file per agent so edits cannot collide, self tests that are machine checkable rather than a summary I have to trust, and a verification layer whose default is to refute. Bulk extraction routes to cheap models on purpose. Judgment, wording and anything touching production stay with me.

The reason I trust it is that it has caught me. It recounted my own data and told me the numbers reproduced but my stated mechanism was wrong, and I had to correct it before it shipped. A system that fact checks its owner is worth more than one that agrees with him.
